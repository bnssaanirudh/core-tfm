## Contribution summary

Recent work shows that predictions from a single frozen tabular foundation model can violate marginalization and factorization consistency. This manuscript studies the downstream repair problem rather than re-diagnosing inconsistency. It formulates four-view post-hoc reconciliation, introduces hard and soft information-projection variants, and uses validation-gated Selective CoRe to avoid imposing a consistency tax when unrepaired or pooled predictions score better. Controlled known-truth experiments isolate view reliability and show when marginal preservation helps or hurts; a heterogeneous task-mixture benchmark evaluates selection against a full candidate-family oracle. The final submission will additionally contain the preregistered released-model benchmark on TabPFN-3, TabICLv2, and TabFM.
