## Contribution summary

Recent work shows that predictions from a single frozen tabular foundation model can violate marginalization and factorization consistency. This manuscript studies the downstream repair problem. It formulates four-view post-hoc reconciliation and evaluates hard, soft, and validation-selected policies. Controlled known-truth experiments isolate when reconciliation helps. A completed bounded benchmark over ten datasets and two released TFMs provides an important negative result: Selective CoRe reduces marginal distortion but is significantly worse than arithmetic pooling on proper scores, with opposite effects on TabICLv2 and TabPFN-3. The paper therefore contributes a reliability-dependent account of the consistency--accuracy trade-off rather than an unconditional performance claim.
