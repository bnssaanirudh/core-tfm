\begin{abstract}
Tabular foundation models (TFMs) can expose direct marginal and conditional probabilities that are not representable by one common joint distribution. We study post-hoc reconciliation of four views, $p(A\mid X)$, $p(B\mid X)$, $p(A\mid B,X)$, and $p(B\mid A,X)$, without retraining the underlying predictor. Hard CoRe preserves direct marginals exactly; Soft CoRe relaxes marginal preservation through KL penalties; Selective CoRe uses held-out labels to choose among raw factorizations, pooling, and reconciliation. Controlled known-truth experiments show that exact marginal preservation helps when direct marginals are reliable and can be strongly harmful when they are not. Across 100 heterogeneous controlled tasks, Selective CoRe improves over arithmetic pooling by $0.006660$ mean exact NLL, winning 68/100 tasks ($p=1.25\times10^{-11}$), with mean full-family oracle regret $0.000710$. The conclusion reverses in a bounded real-model benchmark covering ten data sets, five folds, and two released TFMs: Selective CoRe is worse than arithmetic pooling by $0.001831$ joint NLL (95\% dataset-bootstrap CI $[0.000542,0.003291]$; Wilcoxon $p=0.02734$), while reducing marginal distortion. The effect reverses by upstream model. These results show that compatibility, calibration, and predictive correctness are distinct properties and motivate reconciliation as a reliability-dependent decision rather than a mandatory projection step.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A frozen tabular foundation model can be queried for several probability views of the same targets. For two categorical targets $A$ and $B$, consider direct marginals $p(A\mid X)$ and $p(B\mid X)$ and conditional predictions $p(A\mid B,X)$ and $p(B\mid A,X)$. These views imply two chain-rule joints,
\begin{align}
\Jone(a,b)&=p_B(b)p_{A\mid B}(a\mid b),\\
\Jtwo(a,b)&=p_A(a)p_{B\mid A}(b\mid a).
\end{align}
Exact compatibility requires $\Jone=\Jtwo$ and consistent marginalization. Recent work shows that learned conditional systems, including TFMs, need not satisfy these constraints \citep{klotergens2026agree,majid2025consistent}.

The obvious response is to repair the incompatibility. The difficulty is that any normalized joint is internally coherent by construction. Zero post-repair inconsistency therefore says nothing by itself about calibration or predictive correctness. A mathematically coherent distribution may have worse log loss, worse Brier score, or distorted dependence. This distinction is increasingly important as TFMs are evaluated with proper scoring rules and used in uncertainty-sensitive downstream tasks \citep{gneiting2007strictly,landsgesell2026scoringbench,johnson2026uq}.

CoRe-TFM studies reconciliation as an information-projection and model-selection problem. Arithmetic and geometric pools provide principled KL-barycenter baselines. Hard CoRe projects a consensus distribution onto the transportation polytope defined by the two direct marginals. Soft CoRe replaces exact marginal constraints with KL penalties. Selective CoRe treats reconciliation as optional: validation data may select a raw factorization, a simple pool, or a repair.

The central research question is therefore not ``can incompatibility be removed?'' but ``when does removing incompatibility improve the predictive distribution?'' Our evidence gives a deliberately conditional answer.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We formulate four-view TFM reconciliation while explicitly separating compatibility, calibration, and predictive correctness.
\item We study Hard and Soft CoRe objectives, including a four-view KL decomposition and generalized matrix-scaling solution.
\item We introduce Selective CoRe as finite-candidate validation selection rather than compulsory repair.
\item Controlled exact-view experiments identify the reliability assumptions under which marginal preservation helps or hurts.
\item A heterogeneous 100-task benchmark shows that adaptive selection can outperform a fixed pool when sufficient validation evidence is available.
\item A completed bounded real benchmark over ten data sets, two TFMs, five folds, and a non-TFM boundary baseline shows the opposite regime: the 48-candidate selector loses to arithmetic pooling despite reducing marginal distortion.
\item We document model-specific sign reversal, candidate-family selection cost, validation-size effects, complete provenance, and an explicit robustness protocol for the remaining JMLR-facing experiments.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
TabPFN and related prior-data fitted networks established pretrained in-context prediction for tabular data \citep{hollmann2025tabpfn}. More recent systems including TabPFN-3 and TabICLv2 extend this paradigm in scale and inference behavior \citep{grinsztajn2026tabpfn3,qu2026tabiclv2}. Distributional evaluation of TFMs increasingly emphasizes proper scoring and reliability rather than point accuracy alone \citep{landsgesell2026scoringbench,izbicki2026cde,johnson2026uq}.

The closest diagnostic work studies consistency of conditional predictions \citep{klotergens2026agree}. Compatibility of separately specified conditionals is substantially older; incompatible conditionals can define compromises without corresponding to one exact joint \citep{mure2017compromise}. CoRe-TFM does not claim to introduce conditional compatibility. Its contribution is a post-hoc four-view reconciliation and reliability analysis for frozen predictors.

Hard CoRe is an information projection onto prescribed marginals, closely related to iterative proportional fitting and Bregman projections \citep{benamou2015bregman}. Sinkhorn scaling and generalized scaling for relaxed marginals provide the relevant numerical machinery \citep{cuturi2013sinkhorn,chizat2018scaling}. Algorithmic novelty is not claimed for these primitives.

\section{Problem Formulation}
For categorical $A\in\{1,\ldots,K_A\}$ and $B\in\{1,\ldots,K_B\}$, define the factorization inconsistency
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{fac}}(x)=\TV(\Jone,\Jtwo)=\frac12\sum_{a,b}|\Jone(a,b)-\Jtwo(a,b)|.
\end{equation}
Each factorization preserves one direct marginal automatically and may violate the other. The target of reconciliation is a normalized joint $Q(A,B\mid X)$ that trades off agreement with the two factorized joints, fidelity to the direct marginals, and predictive quality.

The four views overlap statistically because they are produced by repeated queries to the same frozen model; they are not independent Bayesian experts. The objectives below should therefore be interpreted as regularized compatibility objectives, not evidence fusion under conditional independence.

\section{CoRe-TFM}
\subsection{KL-barycenter baselines}
For $w\in[0,1]$,
\begin{align}
Q_{\mathrm{arith},w}&=w\Jone+(1-w)\Jtwo,\\
M_w(a,b)&=\frac{\Jone(a,b)^w\Jtwo(a,b)^{1-w}}{\sum_{a',b'}\Jone(a',b')^w\Jtwo(a',b')^{1-w}}.
\end{align}
The arithmetic pool minimizes $w\KL(\Jone\Vert Q)+(1-w)\KL(\Jtwo\Vert Q)$; the geometric pool minimizes the corresponding reverse-KL barycenter objective.

\subsection{Hard CoRe}
Let
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}(p_A,p_B)=\left\{Q\ge0:\sum_bQ(a,b)=p_A(a),\;\sum_aQ(a,b)=p_B(b)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Hard CoRe is
\begin{equation}
\Qhard=\arg\min_{Q\in\mathcal{T}(p_A,p_B)}\KL(Q\Vert M_w).
\end{equation}
The feasible set is nonempty because $p_Ap_B$ is feasible. For positive $M_w$, strict convexity yields a unique solution. Iterative proportional fitting solves the finite problem.

\subsection{Soft CoRe}
Hard preservation can preserve an inaccurate marginal. Soft CoRe instead solves
\begin{align}
\Qsoft=\arg\min_Q\;&w\KL(Q\Vert\Jone)+(1-w)\KL(Q\Vert\Jtwo)\nonumber\\
&+\lambda_A\KL(Q_A\Vert p_A)+\lambda_B\KL(Q_B\Vert p_B).
\end{align}
The KL chain rule gives
\begin{align}
\KL(Q\Vert\Jone)&=\KL(Q_B\Vert p_B)+\E_{b\sim Q_B}\KL(Q_{A\mid B=b}\Vert p_{A\mid B=b}),\\
\KL(Q\Vert\Jtwo)&=\KL(Q_A\Vert p_A)+\E_{a\sim Q_A}\KL(Q_{B\mid A=a}\Vert p_{B\mid A=a}),
\end{align}
so all four views enter the objective. With positive $M_w$, generalized Sinkhorn-style updates use
\begin{equation}
\tau_A=\frac{\lambda_A}{1+\lambda_A},\qquad \tau_B=\frac{\lambda_B}{1+\lambda_B}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Selective CoRe}
Selective CoRe searches a finite validation-selected family containing the two raw orders, arithmetic pooling, weighted geometric pooling, weighted Hard CoRe, and weighted Soft CoRe. With five direction weights and seven marginal penalties, duplicate removal leaves 48 candidates.

For bounded log loss with probability floor $\epsilon$, $B=-\log\epsilon$, and $M=|\Cset|$, standard finite-class concentration gives
\begin{equation}
L(\hat Q)\le \min_{Q\in\Cset}L(Q)+2B\sqrt{\frac{\log(2M/\delta)}{2n_v}}
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\delta$. The bound is conservative but highlights a key practical dependence: selection becomes harder as candidate-family complexity grows relative to validation size.

\section{Experimental Design}
We use four evidence layers. A surrogate multiclass DGP provides exact $P^*(A,B\mid X)$. An exact-view perturbation benchmark independently corrupts direct marginals and conditional families, isolating the reliability assumptions encoded by reconciliation. A 100-task heterogeneous mixture independently varies the reliability of all four views and evaluates Selective CoRe against a full-family oracle. A validation-size experiment studies finite-validation selection regret.

The real benchmark evaluates Anneal, Car, Credit, Customer, Diamonds, Marketing, MIC, Nursery, Phishing, and Wine. Five deterministic outer folds use at most 256 training rows, 52 disjoint validation rows, and 128 test rows. Primary TFMs are TabICLv2 2.1.1 and TabPFN 8.4.0 through its TabPFN-3 interface. CatBoost 1.2.10 is a non-TFM boundary baseline. TabFM was excluded through an outcome-blind operational amendment because the released implementation was incompatible with the bounded Colab protocol and prohibitively slow.

The real matrix contains 150 dataset--model--fold tasks and 1,200 method rows. The primary inferential unit is the dataset: five folds are averaged within model, then TabICLv2 and TabPFN-3 are averaged within dataset. CatBoost is excluded from the primary TFM claim. Thirty-cell analyses are descriptive only.

\subsection{Model heterogeneity and consistency tax}
The average effect reverses by model: Selective CoRe is worse than arithmetic by 0.006694 NLL on TabICLv2 and better by 0.003031 on TabPFN-3. The paired difference between model-specific effects is 0.009725 ($p=0.00977$; exploratory). This is evidence that reconciliation value depends on the upstream model rather than on inconsistency magnitude alone.

Hard CoRe nearly eliminates marginal distortion but is worse than arithmetic by 0.02056 NLL on every primary dataset ($p=0.00195$). The independent joint is also exactly marginal-preserving and worse by 0.13674 NLL. Geometric pooling is descriptively 0.000492 better than arithmetic, but the paired comparison is not significant ($p=0.7695$).

\subsection{Candidate-family and real validation ablations}
Pool-only selection has mean NLL 1.375591 across the 30 active cells, compared with 1.376046 for the full family, 1.376390 for repair-only selection, and 1.378435 for raw-only selection. The 48-candidate family therefore incurs measurable finite-validation cost.

Using 25\%, 50\%, and 100\% of the fixed 52-example validation pool gives mean NLL 1.375074, 1.376495, and 1.376046, respectively. This real-data sensitivity is non-monotone and should not be used to claim that more validation labels always improve the selector.

\section{Reliability-Aware Extension Protocol}
The audited evidence motivates a second-stage protocol organized around three quantities: available oracle opportunity, selection regret, and view reliability. The repository now includes an evidence-audit script and a dedicated Colab notebook, \texttt{CoRe\_TFM\_JMLR\_ROBUSTNESS\_EXPERIMENTS.ipynb}. The planned fresh-inference studies are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item five independent constrained-sampling seeds with the bounded 256/128 protocol;
\item training-context sensitivity at 64, 128, 256, 512, and 1024 examples where supported;
\item rare-class sensitivity, including Customer, Marketing, and Nursery, whose minimum target-class support is at most five observations;
\item complexity-aware Safe Selective CoRe using nested candidate families and validation-only complexity penalties;
\item per-view direct-marginal and conditional scoring plus per-example factorization and marginalization diagnostics;
\item inference-perturbation dispersion, reported as predictive dispersion/inference instability unless validated against held-out error; and
\item a third released TFM only after an operational and licensing preflight succeeds.
\end{enumerate}
These are protocol commitments, not completed results. No manuscript claim should be updated from them until a new evidence package passes its audit gate.

\section{Discussion}
The controlled and real experiments are not contradictory. The controlled mixture shows that adaptive reconciliation can exploit heterogeneous reliability when the validation budget is large enough. The real bounded benchmark shows that the same flexibility can overfit when 48 candidates are selected from only 52 validation observations. The appropriate deployment question is therefore conditional: is there enough repair opportunity, and is there enough evidence to identify it reliably?

The model-specific sign reversal strengthens this interpretation. A single inconsistency statistic cannot encode which direct or conditional view is trustworthy. The most useful future reconciliation policy should therefore combine structural diagnostics with evidence about view reliability and selection complexity.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
The formulation is pairwise and categorical; global multi-target compatibility and mixed continuous targets are deferred. The primary real benchmark contains only two genuine TFMs. CatBoost is a non-TFM boundary baseline. All current real folds use one deterministic constrained sampling seed and at most 256 training rows. Multi-seed and context-size robustness are therefore required before a final JMLR submission.

Rare target classes are another important limitation. The audited admission preflight identifies minimum target-class support of 3 for Customer, 2 for Marketing, and 2 for Nursery; support-sensitive analyses are required. The selector searches 48 candidates with only 52 validation observations and empirically pays a selection cost. ECE is descriptive and conclusions rely primarily on proper scores. Finally, the controlled reliability mixture is a designed stress test, not an estimate of the natural prevalence of reliability regimes in deployed TFMs.

\section{Reproducibility}
The frozen bounded evidence package contains 1,200 fold-level method rows, 150 completed fold tasks, controlled replications, selection ablations, validation sensitivity, fold manifests, dataset hashes, source revisions, package versions, hardware metadata, plots, and SHA-256 checksums. The archived run used Python 3.13.15, PyTorch 2.11.0 with CUDA 12.8, TabICL 2.1.1, TabPFN 8.4.0, CatBoost 1.2.10, and a Tesla T4. The execution snapshot is commit \texttt{ec6ff45d709096a1665f2475840c466587bc3d26}. The repository-native evidence auditor recomputes the dataset-blocked effects and checks the expected 1,200-row matrix and checksum manifest. The 30-cell statistics table is descriptive only; primary confirmatory interpretation uses datasets after averaging the two TFMs.

\section{Conclusion}
CoRe-TFM shows that probability coherence is not a surrogate for probability accuracy. Exact marginal preservation helps when those marginals are reliable and can fail sharply when they are not. Adaptive selection succeeds in controlled heterogeneous reliability regimes with ample validation evidence, but the bounded real benchmark shows that a large selector can lose to simple arithmetic pooling while still reducing structural distortion. The effect also reverses between TabICLv2 and TabPFN-3. The defensible conclusion is therefore reliability-aware: reconcile only when the expected repair opportunity and validation evidence justify the additional complexity.

\section{Additional Derivations}
For normalized distributions $P_1,P_2$, minimizing $w\KL(P_1\Vert Q)+(1-w)\KL(P_2\Vert Q)$ yields the arithmetic pool. Reversing the KL arguments yields the normalized weighted geometric pool. The Soft-CoRe chain-rule decomposition follows directly by writing $Q(a,b)=Q_B(b)Q_{A\mid B}(a\mid b)$ and expanding the logarithm.

\section{Reporting Guardrails}
The four views are overlapping predictive queries from one frozen model and are not treated as independent evidence sources. Test truth is never used for deployable candidate selection. Candidate-family oracles are analysis-only. The 30 dataset--model cells are descriptive; primary cross-model inference is dataset-blocked after averaging the two TFMs. Fresh robustness experiments must be archived under a new protocol revision rather than written into the frozen Q1 result directory.
